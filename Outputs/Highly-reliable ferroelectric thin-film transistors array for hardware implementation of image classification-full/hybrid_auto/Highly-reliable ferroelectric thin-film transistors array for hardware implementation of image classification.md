# a b s t r a c t

Ferroelectric thin film transistors (FeTFTs) have attracted great attention for in-memory computing applications due to low power consumption and monolithic three-dimensional integration capability. Herein, we propose a planar integrated highly-reliable metal-ferroelectric-metal-insulator-semiconductor FeTFTs device, in which the weak erase issue is suppressed by implanting a floating gate, and the interface defects are reduced by simplifying the fabrication process. These lead to significant improvements in device performance, including large memory window (4.3 V), high conductance dynamic range (1400), high endurance (10^12), and low variation (cycle-to-cycle: 2.5 %/device-to-device: 3.5 %). Moreover, we fabricated a 16 x 16 FeTFTs pseudo-crossbar array for in-memory computing and experimentally demonstrated full hardware implementation of multi-layer perceptron for the classification of four fundamental arithmetic operation symbols. This work provides a potential hardware solution for implementing a highly-efficient in-memory computing system based on highly-reliable FeTFTs array.

# 1. Introduction

ChatGPT has garnered significant attention due to its potential applications in science, education, and society [1]. Its high intelligence stems from a large natural language model with 175 billion parameters [2]. Up until now, the development of artificial intelligence (AI) technology has heavily relied on the expansion of artificial neural networks, leading to increasing power consumption. Based on the statistical projections, the electricity needed to train a leading AI model is projected to surpass the global annual power consumption by 2030 [3]. The extensively used AI hardware, such as graphics processing units (GPUs) and tensor processing units (TPUs), are built on the von Neumann computing architecture, which separates memory from logic and faces significant challenges in improving power efficiency [4,5]. Substantial improvements in the power efficiency of AI hardware will be urgently required.

In-memory computing with non-volatile memory (NVM) crossbar/pseudo-crossbar arrays has emerged as a new computing paradigm for highly efficient AI hardware, where computation is performed at a memory unit without the need for data transfer [6-8]. By applying Ohm's law and Kirchhoff's law, vector-matrix multiplication (VMM) can be achieved in a single read operation cycle using a crossbar or pseudo-crossbar array [9,10]. Compared to traditional VMM hardware implemented by multiple adders, the computing power efficiency can be improved by one to two orders of magnitude [11,12]. This improvement has great potential for an efficient neural network accelerator. Although the VMM operation based on NVM array is well-suited for accelerating neural networks, it is unable to achieve precise computing and instruction control in general-purpose processors. The collaborative processing architecture between a general-purpose processor and VMM hardware is a research hotspot [13-17].

Various emerging NVM devices have been extensively studied to validate in-memory computing architectures, including two-terminal resistive switching memories [18-20], phase change memories [21,22], magnetic tunnel junctions [23,24], ferroelectric memories [25-28], and three-terminal charge-trapping transistors [29,30], as well as ferroelectric transistors [31-36]. Three-terminal devices have the advantage over two-terminal devices of separating the read and write paths, which allows to write to a high-impedance gate with low power consumption [37,38]. Among them, ferroelectric thin-film transistors (FeTFTs) based on zirconium doped hafnium oxide have attracted increasing attention due to their low power consumption, high speed, ability to support multiple states, and monolithic three-dimensional (M3D) integration [39-45]. Metal oxide semiconductors, such as indium tungsten oxide (IWO) and indium gallium zinc oxide (IGZO), are commonly used as channel materials in FeTFTs [46-52]. Compared to traditional silicon channels [34,35], the low leakage current and low fabrication cost of large areas of IGZO films make it potential to develop low-power devices and large-scale integration, which can be deposited through the back-end-of-line (BEOL) processes, ensuring a small thermal budget to avoid affecting the underlying devices. Thus, FeTFTs can be heterogeneously integrated with traditional complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) devices using an M3D structure, which represents the optimal collaborative processing architecture between a general-purpose processor and VMM hardware. However, significant challenges of FeTFTs are the weak erase issue caused by non-uniform electric field distribution during the erase process [47,53,54], as well as reliability degradation resulting from the defects at the ferroelectric/semiconductor interface [55-60]. All of these lead to poor memory window and reliability. Moreover, the application of FeTFT arrays in neural networks is still in the stage of partial function realization [41]. The research of full hardware implementation of in-memory computing systems based on highly reliable FeTFTs arrays is of great significance.

In this study, a highly reliable metal-ferroelectric-metal-insulator-semiconductor (MFMIS) FeTFTs pseudo-crossbar array was fabricated, and board-level circuit hardware systems were demonstrated with image classification capability. In contrast to the conventional vertically integrated MFMIS structure devices [60-63], a planar integrated MFMIS-FeTFT with ferroelectric capacitor and transistor laterally connected through the floating gate was employed. The planar integrated MFMIS-FeTFT structure not only mitigates weak erase issues but also substantially simplifies the fabrication process and reduces interface defects. These lead to a significant improvement in device performance, including high conductance ratio (G_max/G_min > 1400), long retention time (10^6 s), high endurance (10^12 cycles), low cycle-to-cycle and device-to-device variation (2.5 %/3.5 %), and low energy consumption (47.6 fJ per spike). These characteristics present the potential for high power efficiency and high precision in-memory computing applications. In addition, we constructed 16 16 FeTFTs pseudo-crossbar array for in-memory computing and experimentally demonstrated the full hardware implementation of multi-layer perceptron (MLP) for the classification of four fundamental arithmetic operation symbols. The FeTFTs-based system demonstrates over two orders of magnitude improvement in power efficiency for image classification compared to typical CMOS systems. Meanwhile, simulations of neural networks based on the characteristics of FeTFTs were carried out by using the Modified National Institute of Standards and Technology (MNIST) handwritten digit database, which demonstrated an image recognition accuracy of 96.6 %. This validates the potential of FeTFTs in large-scale network applications. This work presents a practical strategy for implementing a high-performance in-memory computing hardware system based on a highly reliable FeTFTs array.

# 2. Results and discussion

# 2.1. Fabrication and electric characteristics of the FeTFTs

In n-type oxide semiconductor FeTFTs with metal-ferroelectric-semiconductor (MFS) structure, the weak erase operation can result in substantial asymmetry and nonlinearity of analog synaptic devices. This is due to the insufficient holes in the channel counterbalancing the polarization charge when a negative voltage is applied to the gate electrode during the erase process. This results in an uneven distribution of the electric field during the erase process which hinders the switching of the ferroelectric domain [53]. Fig. 1(a) illustrates the simulated distribution of electrostatic potential by using Sentaurus technology computer-aided design (TCAD) for MFS FeTFTs and MFMIS FeTFTs at 4 V, respectively. Fig. 1(b) illustrates the distribution of electrostatic potential as denoted by the red dashed line in Fig. 1(a). In the conventional MFS FeTFTs, the electrostatic potential distribution in the ferroelectric layer was non-uniform when a negative voltage was applied. This finding aligns with our previous analytical findings and the conclusions in other published pieces of literature [47,54]. The insertion of a metal layer in MFMIS FeTFTs serves to evenly distribute the lateral potential in the ferroelectric layer under negative voltage, thereby addressing the inherent weak erase problem. Moreover, the MFMIS FeTFTs efficiently mitigate the charge trapping and reliability degradation resulting from the direct contact between ferroelectric and semiconductor. Notably, it is possible to flexibly engineer the area of ferroelectric capacitor, offering a means to modify the area matching between ferroelectric capacitor and thin film transistors (TFTs). Therefore, MFMIS FeTFTs possess a distinct competitive advantage in terms of program/erase symmetry and reliability.

Fig. 1(c) presents a schematic illustration of the fabricated FeTFTs. The detailed fabrication processes are outlined in the method section, which is compatible with BEOL processes. The ferroelectric capacitors and transistors were planar integrated through floating gate electrodes. Different from the traditional vertically integrated structure, in which the ferroelectric capacitor is vertically integrated at the gate terminal of the transistor, the planar integrated MFMIS-FeTFT can significantly simplify the fabrication process, save 3 lithography masks, and reduce interface defects caused by the process. A schematic diagram of the MFMIS-FeTFT preparation process for both planar and vertical integration is shown in Fig. S1 in supplementary material. Excessive lithography process can degrade interface quality and increase interface defects between ferroelectric and metal layer. Additionally, planar structure devices feature fewer steps, effectively mitigating manufacturing challenges associated with large step heights, such as interlayer alignment and interface quality control, which can significantly reduce the generation of defects at steps. Fig. 1(d) depicts the topview optical microscope image of the fabricated FeTFTs, where the ferroelectric capacitor (depicted as a blue square) was integrated with the TFT. A_DE and A_FE represent the area of the channel in the TFT (depicted as a white box) and ferroelectric capacitor (depicted as a blue box), respectively. The cross-sectional transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images of the TFT and ferroelectric capacitor are depicted in Fig. 1(e, f). Energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) mapping and line scan were utilized to comprehensively analyze the elements distribution (Fig. 1(g, h)). The EDS mapping and line scan of the ferroelectric capacitor were also confirmed (Fig. S2). The thicknesses of TiN/HfZrO_x/IGZO/Ti/Au layers are 20, 15, 15, 5, and 45 nm, respectively.

The ferroelectric properties of the TiN/HfZrO_x/Ti/Au ferroelectric capacitor were initially confirmed (Fig. S3). The remnant polarization of the ferroelectric capacitor exhibited an increase as the voltage sweep range expanded from 2 to 3.5 V. At a voltage amplitude of 3.5 V, the ferroelectric capacitor could attain a maximum remnant polarization of 10.5 mu C cm^-2. When the scanning voltage exceeded 3.5 V, a small gap induced by leakage current would appear at 0 V, and this phenomenon did not affect the performance in MFMIS FeTFTs. The results indicate that the polarization characteristics are tunable by altering the applied voltage amplitudes. The electrical performances of the TFT were evaluated in direct current mode (Fig. S4). The transfer curves (I_D-V_G) and output curves (I_D-V_DS) of the IGZO TFT with a channel length of 10 um exhibit a large on/off ratio, low operating voltage, and low gate leakage current.

In the circuit model of the MFMIS FeTFTs, a series voltage division relationship exists between the ferroelectric capacitor and the gate dielectric capacitor of the TFT. The voltages across the ferroelectric (V_FE) and dielectric layer (V_DE) of the TFT exhibit an inverse relationship with the capacitance. By decreasing the area ratio (A_FE/A_DE) between the ferroelectric capacitor and the TFT, it is possible to elevate the V_FE to augment the switching probability of the ferroelectric domain, thereby enhancing the memory window (MW) and conductance dynamic range. The transfer characteristics of the MFMIS FeTFTs were evaluated with various A_FE/A_DE ratios of 0.0625, 0.25, 1, 2.25, and 4 (Fig. 2(a)). The transfer curves exhibit typical counterclockwise hysteresis characteristics, which can be attributed to the presence of ferroelectric polarization. The MW, defined as the difference between positive and negative threshold voltage (V_th), rises from 2.2 to 4.6 V as the A_FE/A_DE ratio decreases from 4 to 0.0625. The statistical results from 20 devices of each type are depicted in Fig. 2(b), indicating that the memory performance of the fabricated MFMIS FeTFTs is effectively enhanced through the regulation of A_FE/A_DE.

By precisely modulating domain switching in a ferroelectric capacitor, the channel conductance is adjusted through multilevel polarization. The MFMIS FeTFTs demonstrate multiple state operations. An amplitude rise of the applied voltage causes an increased domain switching, leading to high electron concentration accumulated in the channel, which further affords multiple conductance states (Fig. S5). Therefore, the channel conductance could be readily adjusted by the amplitude of applied pulses. The potentiation and depression processes were examined at various A_FE/A_DE ratios while applying incremental amplitude pulses (2 to 5 V, 50 mV step) (Fig. S6). The conductance was measured by applying read voltages (V_G = 0 V, V_DS = 0.1 V) after each pulse. The findings indicate that the conductance dynamic range exhibits a gradual increase as the A_FE/A_DE ratio decreases. Nevertheless, the device with an A_FE/A_DE ratio of 0.25 demonstrates superior performance in various comprehensive aspects, including conductance modulation linearity, symmetry, and conductance dynamic range. Therefore, it has been selected for further investigation. Moreover, in order to examine the uniformity and stability of the devices, the transfer curves of 40 devices with A_FE/A_DE ratios of 0.25 were measured. Fig. 2(c) presents the statistical analysis of the V_th for 40 devices, demonstrating a normal distribution. The specific test results for cycle-to-cycle and device-to-device variations are presented in Fig. S7. The statistical analysis involved the distribution of high and low V_th over 40 cycles of 40 devices. The results suggest that FeTFTs can be achieved with minimal variation.

The pulse scheme plays a crucial role in modulating multiple states. The modulation laws of synaptic potentiation properties under varying pulse width and pulse amplitude are examined in Fig. 2(d, e). Before the measurement, a gate bias of 4 V was applied to modulate the device to a consistently low conductance level. The potentiation pulse widths increased from 100 ns to 400 us with the pulse amplitude fixed at 3 V, resulting in a reduction in the number of pulses needed to achieve maximum conductance (Fig. 2(d)). Maintaining the pulse widths at a constant 100 us, ensuring sufficient conductance dynamic range and multiple states, the pulse amplitude was adjusted within the range of 2 to 4.5 V. A similar trend in the conductance profile was observed (Fig. 2(e)), suggesting that high amplitude pulses reduce the pulses number needed to maximize the conductance. An identical procedure was conducted for depression pulses (Fig. S8). The findings indicate that both pulse width and amplitude exert a positive correlation on conductance modulation, with the pulse amplitude demonstrating a more pronounced effect.

Efficient in-memory computing systems require synaptic characteristics with multiple states, high conductance dynamic range (G_max/G_min), linear conductance modulation, and low device-to-device/cycle-to-cycle variation. To investigate the potentiation and depression properties of FeTFTs, multiple pulses with incremental amplitudes (2 to 4.6 V, 10 mV step) and a width of 100 us were applied to the gate (Fig. 2(f)) according to the optimized pulse scheme from Fig. 2(d, e). The FeTFTs exhibit favorable potentiation and depression properties, including high G_max/G_min (1400), multiple states (256 levels), and low nonlinearity (alpha_p = 0.49, alpha_d = -1.72, alpha_p and alpha_d represent nonlinearity of potentiation/depression, respectively). The FeTFTs exhibit linear current-voltage (I_D-V_DS) characteristics at multiple states within the voltage ranging from 0 to 0.3 V (Fig. S9). In addition, it has the capability to accommodate both pulse amplitude and pulse width modulation schemes for in-memory computing applications. Then the consecutive operation of 3600 pulses over 30 cycles was examined (Fig. 2(g)). The results indicate that the FeTFTs in multiple states demonstrate strong cycle consistency. The error bars demonstrate a modest cycle-to-cycle variation of 2.5 % and a device-to-device variation of 3.5 % over 30 cycles and 30 devices (Fig. S10).

The stability was assessed by testing the retention and endurance performance of the FeTFTs, as shown in Fig. 2(h, i), respectively. The results indicate that the average conductance loss is approximately 15 % compared with the initial state after a 10^6-second test duration, while the average conductance loss is around 4 % for 10^5 s. Despite an increase in decay over time, the high and low conductance states of the device remain within a window exceeding two orders of magnitude after 10^6 s. The multilevel retention properties suggest that FeTFTs have the potential for in-memory computing. Prior to conducting endurance testing, the operating speed of the FeTFTs was characterized. The correlations between the conductance associated with program/erase state and pulse widths were investigated, where the pulse amplitudes were 3, 3.5, and 4 V, respectively (Fig. S11). The results show a conductance window larger than an order of magnitude can still be observed when the pulse width is reduced to 50 ns, indicating that the devices are capable of achieving a maximum programming and erasing speed of 50 ns with a 3.5 V operation pulse, and the energy consumption of the FeTFTs for a single operation is as low as 47.6 fJ/spike. The competitive advantage of low power consumption is achieved by separating the read and write paths of the three-terminal transistors. Fig. 2(i) depicts the endurance characteristics with varied pulse amplitudes of 3, 3.5, and 4 V with the first 100 cycles recorded in Fig. S12. To optimize test time, a single programming and erase pulse with 100 ns width was employed to modulate the conductance state. This approach does not fully achieve ferroelectric polarization switching, resulting in a relatively narrow conductance range. However, it can assess the ability of the conductance to be continuously adjustable under cyclic pulse excitation, which reflects the device's capacity to withstand continuous ferroelectric polarization switches. The endurance surpasses 10^12 cycles with a gate pulse amplitude of 3 V, which is partly attributed to the effect of mitigating trapped interface charges by inserting a metal layer in MFMIS FeTFTs and the process simplification brought by the planar integrated structure. In a word, the FeTFTs exhibit excellent electrical performance and readily meet the inference and recognition criteria for in-memory computing.

# 2.2. Ferroelectric thin film transistors array

The FeTFTs array featuring MFMIS structure cells was fabricated through BEOL processes (Fig. S13). The detailed fabrication processes are outlined in the method section. The FeTFTs array was constructed through a cross-interconnection scheme and comprised 16 word lines (WLs), 16 source lines (SLs), and 16 bit lines (BLs). The gate and source of the FeTFTs within the same column were connected to WLs and SLs, while the drain of the FeTFTs within the same row was connected to BLs. The overall electrical performance of the FeTFTs array was assessed by assembling the chip dies from as-fabricated wafer (Fig. 3(a)). Fig. 3(b) presents the optical microscope image of a 16 16 FeTFTs array chip die. The custom-designed test circuit board was created to facilitate the electrical measurement of FeTFTs array. The circuit board served to provide peripheral functions, including voltage pulse generation, decoding and switch matrix, and current sensing. The transimpedance amplifier (TIA) circuit was employed to convert the output current into voltage within the current sensing circuit.

Fig. 3(c) illustrates the operation scheme of the FeTFTs array. In the FeTFTs array, the devices within the same column shared a WL. Therefore, updating the conductance will inevitably lead to an undesired operation disturbance of unselected devices within the same column. In this paper, we proposed a V_op/2 operation scheme to mitigate the cross-talk issues. The operation pulses (V_op) with incremental amplitudes (50 mV step) were applied to the WL of selected devices, in order to achieve both potentiation (2 to 4 V) and depression (-2 to -4 V) behaviors. The BL and SL of the selected devices were set to 0 V and V_op/2, respectively. Synchronous pulses of V_op/2 were applied to unselected BLs. The WLs and SLs of the unselected device were both set to 0 V. The conductance was confirmed by measuring the current of BLs with a read voltage of 0.2 V applied to the selected SL, while the remaining terminals were all set to 0 V.

The V_op/2 operation scheme was employed to measure the conductance update behavior of the selected device and the operation cross-talk behavior of the unselected device, as shown in Fig. 3(d, e). The selected devices demonstrate a conductance update behavior with linear potentiation and depression characteristics. It should be noted that the observed conductance range is constrained by the measurement precision of the test circuit board. Meanwhile, the conductance values of the unselected devices within the same column exhibit slight changes (Fig. 3(d)). The in-memory computing architecture can accommodate this due to its fuzzy computing paradigm and fault tolerance capability. The V_op/2 operation scheme in this FeTFTs array was unable to eliminate operation disturbance completely, and incorporating an additional selector device was an effective solution. Fig. 3(e) demonstrates the operation cross-talk behavior of unselected devices within the same row, and the conductance values are slightly changed, suggesting negligible disturbance issues.

Uniformity and yield are essential metrics for the hardware implementation of in-memory computing systems. Sixteen devices within the 16 x 16 FeTFTs array were randomly chosen to measure the potentiation and depression performance (Fig. 3(f)), which demonstrated outstanding consistency in conductance update behavior, with conductance distributions ranging from 10 to 40 uS. Moreover, the practical results indicate that the yield of the 16 16 FeTFTs array achieves 100 %.

Accurate weight mapping is essential for neural network inference and recognition tasks. A grayscale image depicting the ID-CAS logo, an abbreviation for our research team, was utilized to map different conductance states (Fig. 3(g)). The grayscale value of the image was linearly mapped to the corresponding conductance (10 to 40 uS). The conductance corresponding to the target image was applied to the 16 16 FeTFTs array, and the mapping of the 64 x 64 pixels pattern was achieved through 16 iterations of the loop. The discrepancy between the target and practical conductance image is subsequently subjected to statistical analysis (Fig. 3(h)), showing that the accuracy of mapping is generally maintained within a 5 % margin. The findings suggest that the FeTFTs array has a high potential to be employed for efficient in-memory computing hardware with high accuracy.

# 2.3. Hardware implementation of MLP based on FeTFTs array

To demonstrate the capabilities of FeTFTs array for in-memory computing, a hardware inference system for MLP was developed using the circuit board detailed in the preceding section. The MLP network was employed to carry out the classification of four mathematical operation patterns ("+", "-", "x", and "/") using a 4 x 4 pixels grid (Fig. 4(a)). The training set comprised 80 randomly selected samples, while 20 samples were set aside for the testing set (detailed data set in Fig. S14). The MLP network was composed of 16 inputs, 10 hidden layer neurons, and 4 outputs. A pair of FeTFTs were employed to depict the positive and negative weights within the neural network. Consequently, three arrays of 16 x 16 FeTFTs were utilized as memory arrays for weights to execute the VMM function of the MLP network. Fig. 4(b) depicts the detailed implementation method of the neural network in the FeTFTs array. The feature vectors of 4 x 4 pixels patterns were specifically linearly mapped to the input pulse amplitude. Concurrently, the network weights were differentially mapped to the conductance of the FeTFTs. The detailed hardware implementation methods are described in Note S1. The conductance map of the two-layer MLP network is depicted in Fig. 4(c). The currents of the hidden neurons and output neurons for the 20 testing samples are depicted in Fig. 4(d). The maximum currents of the output neurons correspond to the data category identified by the network.

The comparison of recognition accuracy was conducted for the mathematical software simulation (S.S.), the behavioral level software simulation (B.S.), and the hardware inference system implemented (H.I.) on the FeTFTs array (Fig. 4(e)). The behavioral level software simulation has demonstrated a 100 % accuracy, comparable to that of the mathematical software simulation. The network implemented using the FeTFTs array demonstrates an accuracy of 95 %, which can be ascribed to the non-ideal characteristics of the FeTFTs array. The findings confirm that the FeTFTs array-based MLP network demonstrates adequate recognition accuracy.

In addition to achieving high recognition accuracy, FeTFTs array-based analog computing systems present an attractive platform for the development of low-power, high-efficiency hardware systems. To assess the performance of FeTFTs array-based system in comparison to a traditional CMOS-based system, the power efficiency of the two systems was estimated (Fig. 4(f)). The power efficiency of a standard CMOS system was approximated to be 328.6 uW/class (as detailed in the method section). The FeTFTs array-based system demonstrates an outstanding power efficiency of 1.7 uW class^-1, demonstrating 193 improvement in power efficiency. The advantage of low power consumption is derived from the architectural advantages of analog computing. The energy efficiency of core processing unit operation utilizing FeTFTs array was calculated to be 159.2 tera-operations per second per watt (TOPS/W), as detailed in Note S2. Simulations were carried out to confirm the performance of a large-scale FeTFTs array in accordance with its characteristics. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) were employed for the analysis of the MNIST dataset. The simulation achieves an image recognition accuracy of 96.6 % (Simulation details are included in Fig. S15 and Note S3). The comprehensive advantages of FeTFTs are clearly demonstrated through a benchmark comparison with other emerging memory devices, as summarized in Table 1.

MFMIS FeTFTs with planar integrated structure exhibit superior performance in key metrics including array size, memory window, conductance dynamic range, multiple states, retention, and endurance. The distinctive advantages of FeTFTs array render an appealing choice for in-memory computing. Moreover, the FeTFTs are compatible with BEOL processes due to the low deposition temperature enabled by the using of oxide channel materials. This feature allows the FeTFTs to be vertically stacked on the Si CMOS logic layer for in-memory computing (Fig. S16). Indeed, the planar integration of ferroelectric capacitors will lose some integration intensity. But planar integrated FeTFT consumes only 25 % more area than vertically integrated devices because ferroelectric capacitors only account for a quarter of the transistor channel area. In monolithic 3D integrated chip applications, the area of traditional silicon-based logic circuits is much larger than FeTFT-integrated circuits. The integration of M3D is expected to emerge as a pivotal solution for meeting the ultra-large-scale demands of system-on-chip, and mitigating the latency associated with computation and memory data exchange for high-throughput on-chip data-rich processing. In conclusion, the planar FeTFTs are promising for large-scale integration due to their high reliability, excellent performance, and M3D integration capability. And it significantly improves device reliability at the cost of a small area penalty.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we experimentally demonstrated a highly reliable FeTFTs device and a highly efficient hardware inference system for image classification. The fabricated FeTFTs exhibit notable adjustability of memory window and conductance dynamic range. The fabricated FeTFTs array exhibits superior performance in terms of uniformity, reliability, conductance dynamic range, and power consumption. All of these are essential for attaining high-precision and high computational power efficiency. The VMM calculation of the FeTFTs array was utilized to execute the fully connected layer calculation of an MLP, effectively accomplishing image classification tasks. The hardware inference system based on FeTFTs array achieved an accuracy of 95 %, representing a nearly 193-fold improvement in power efficiency compared to the traditional CMOS system. Meanwhile, simulations of CNNs based on the characteristics of FeTFTs achieved a 96.6% accuracy in image recognition using the MNIST dataset. This work signifies a notable advancement in the investigation of highly efficient computing solutions. The FeTFTs array-based system has the potential to be extended to more complex task processing in the subsequent research. The M3D integrated chip can be utilized to enhance processing capability, providing a solution for highly reliable neuromorphic in-memory computing applications.

# 4. Experimental section

## 4.1. Simulation method

The simulations were performed with Synopsys Sentaurus TCAD. In the simulations, the Poisson equation, Shockley-Read-Hall recombination, electric-field, and mobility models were employed with consideration of charge trap mechanisms. In the simulation, the remanent polarization value and thickness of HZO were set to 15 uC/cm^2 and 15 nm, the thickness of gate dielectric was 10 nm, the thickness of IGZO channel was 15 nm, and width and length were 200 nm and 50 nm, respectively.

## 4.2. Sample fabrication

The fabrication processes of FeTFTs were as follows. The SiO2/Si substrate was cleaned in acetone, ethanol, and deionized water for 5min each. The floating gate was formed by depositing a 20 nm TiN layer on the SiO2/Si substrate using radio frequency (RF) sputtering. The TiN layer was patterned using the first lithography mask. Then, a 10 nm HfZrOx film was deposited as the ferroelectric layer (FE) of the capacitor and dielectric layer (DE) of the TFT using atomic layer deposition (ALD) at a substrate temperature of 250 C. The Hf[N(C2H5)CH3]4, Zr[N(C2H5)CH3]4, and H2O were used as the hafnium precursor, zirconium precursor and oxygen source, respectively. The hafnium/zirconium ratio was controlled by adjusting the deposition of one cycle of HfO2 and ZrO2. Then, a 15 nm IGZO film was deposited as the channel layer using RF sputtering at room temperature with In/Ga/Zn = 1:1:1. The IGZO layer was patterned using the second lithography mask and the wet etching method. Ti/Au films (5/45 nm) were deposited as source/drain and gate electrodes using electron beam evaporation at room temperature. The channel length and width were 10 um and 100 um, respectively. The devices were then annealed at 400 C for 30 s under N2 gas to induce ferroelectric properties. The fabrication processes of FeTFTs arrays are as follows. After the previous device preparation, Ti/Au (5/45 nm) films were deposited and patterned to connect the gate (WLs) as well as source (SLs) and drain (BLs) electrodes. Subsequently, a 60 nm SiO2 film was deposited as the insulation layer using RF sputtering at room temperature. The lift-off method was used to open the contacts for the gate, source, and drain electrodes. A Ti/Au (5/80 nm) film was deposited and patterned to form BLs using RF at room temperature.

## 4.3. Measurement method

Optical images of the devices were captured using an optical microscope. The cross-section TEM and energy dispersive spectroscopy were analyzed by transmission electron microscopy. The DC mode was measured by Agilent B1500 semiconductor parameter analyzer. B1530A fast measurement unit module was used for generating the voltage pulse and measuring the response current at the same time.

## 4.4. Power efficiency estimation

The power efficiency of the FeTFTs array-based classifier and the traditional CMOS classifier was evaluated. The energy consumption of a standard support vector machine (SVM) typical classifier designed with CMOS technology is 273 uJ for every 2-second signal epoch when the input feature vector is (18 x 3 x 8) [67]. Therefore, the power efficiency is estimated as 273 uJ / 2 s x (16 inputs x 10 inputs) / (18 x 3 x 8 inputs) / (2 class) x 4 class x 10 Hz / (0.5 Hz) = 328.6 uW/class with the input dimensions of the classifier for 16 and 10, and a classification rate of 10 Hz. The average power of the FeTFT reading operation was determined by the average input voltage and the average conductance, and is calculated as (95.4 mV)^2 x 13.8 uS approx 0.12 uW. When three FeTFT arrays were used to achieve 16 x 10 x 2 and 10 x 4 x 2 network weights, the power efficiency expression is [0.12 uW x (16 x 10 x 2 + 10 x 4 x 2) + 0.3 mW x (10 x 2 + 4 x 2)] x 2 x 10^-5 s x 10 Hz = 1.7 uW/class at 10 Hz using a differential pair and with a 20 us computational pulse. Where 0.3 mW is the estimated power of TIA to convert the output current of FeTFTs array (in the typical range from -28 to 28 uA) into voltage [11]. Based on these estimates, the power efficiency of the FeTFTs-based calculator is 193 times higher than that of the CMOS-based calculator.
