Abstract: Scaled ferroelectric FinFET devices were fabricated with post fin formation surface engineering (SE) to remove the line-edge roughness (LER) from the silicon surface by dry etching. This facilitated 3bit/cell operations in $10\mathrm{nm}$ $\mathrm{Hf}_{0.5}\mathrm{Zr}_{0.5}\mathrm{O}_2$ based ferroelectric FinFETs along with on-state current $(I_{ON})$ to off-state current $(I_{OFF})$ ratio of $10^{6}$ , extrapolated 10-year retention and endurance above $10^{11}$ cycles. Further, we have evaluated its performance in all ferroelectric neural network, where ferroelectric FinFETs are used as synaptic devices or neurons for weight storage. Synaptic core built with optimized devices achieve software-comparable $97.91\%$ inference accuracy on MNIST data and multi-layer perceptron network.

Introduction: Recent advents in the research of $\mathrm{Hf_{0.5}Zr_{0.5}O_2}$ (HZO) based ferroelectric (FE) memories have framed it as a promising next generation non-volatile memory owing to its fast-write-speed [1] and high endurance [2]. However, the major drawback lies in its rising infidelity with scaling, resulting device to device (D2D) variation and degradation of endurance in deeply scaled devices, stemmed from charge trapping from channel side (CS). There has been a numerous attempt to mitigate these issues for accelerating further scaling of FE-FET devices. However previous research mostly focused on alleviating only a part of these issues. For example, devices reported in [3-5] displayed multilevel cell (MLC) characteristics, but the limited endurance $(10^{4} - 10^{5}$ cycles) and the high write voltage requirements, renders them inadequate for array-level applications [6]. In this work, we focus on three aspects of FE-FinFETs: infusing MLC, high-endurance and low-power programming. The pivotal predicaments with the degradation in FE-FinFET's performance are twofold. First, the degradation of gate-stack due to the presence of parasitic charge clouds at the interface [7], increases charge-trapping probability from the channel side (CS). Second, LER limits $I_{ON} / I_{OFF}$ , inhibiting fast programming and facilitates charge trapping [8-9]. Therefore, the quality of Si-HZO interface plays a decisive role in the performance of FE-FinFETs. In this work, we attempt to remove the parasitic charge cloud and LER by surface engineering (SE), mainly to remove the etching damaged Si-SiO $_2$ interfacial layer (IL) between Si and HZO. The devices with SE displayed reduced D2D variation, good endurance and retention properties, and 3bit/cell operations.

Experiments and Results: Experiments were started by fabricating the FE-FinFETs on silicon-on-insulator (SOI) wafers in two different splits (Fig.1). Split: 1 had $1.5\mathrm{nm}$ IL of $\mathrm{SiO}_2$ between Si and HZO generated by thermal oxidation, whereas in the second split $\mathrm{SiO}_2$ was used as sacrificial oxide for mitigating the fin-LER and IL trap states formed during the dry-etching with HBr. The XPS analysis (Fig.2(a)) confirmed the presence of bromium ions at $\mathrm{Si - SiO_2}$ interface formed during the dry-etching of Si-fin with HBr, owing to the favorable Gibbs' free energy of Si-Br and H-Br molecules [10], which is removed by SE in Split-2. This process also reduces the fin-LER, confirmed by SEM (Fig.2(b)) and TEM

(Fig.2(c)). The direct impact of interface optimization by SE was captured by flicker noise (Fig.3), where the SE curtailed noise by 3-orders of magnitude and the devices with SE followed the Lorentzian trend whereas the devices without SE followed 1/f trend, suggesting a smaller number of electrically activated traps in the devices at that bias after SE. The SE also improved the $I_{ON} / I_{OFF}$ (Fig.4) as the Si-fin LER was reduced. Parasitic $Br-$ and other interfacial charges from the dangling bonds, which increases D2D (Fig.5(a)), were removed by SE using HF solution and $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2$ , inciting amelioration of D2D variation during programming. After SE, devices had a memory window (MW) of 2V (Fig.5(b)), while the devices without SE did not have significant MW (Fig.5(a)). This trend was further supported in by the overlapping threshold voltage $(V_{th})$ distribution after program-erase operations (Fig.6), which suggests presence of many IL traps prior to SE. Fig.7(a) demonstrates kindling of 3bit/cell operation in the devices after SE with a maximum standard deviation of $10\%$ in each state. Fig.7(b) shows highly stable read-write operations in a single device for 8 distinct states. The global variability of MW (Fig.8) proves poor interface properties, inhibiting the performance of the device prior to SE, which sees a havoc boost after SE. The second significant improvement was the device endurance, measured via the consecutive-program (5V, 100ns), read (0V), consecutive-erase (-5V), and read cycling (Fig.9). Endurance degradation is an aftermath of leakage and trapping, which was improved to a striking value over $10^{11}$ cycles from $10^5$ after SE. Retention characteristics (Fig.10) at $85^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ shows extrapolated data retention up to 10 years, suggesting negligible impact of interface charge induced depolarization field. We have also evaluated the performance of the FE-FinFETs in large-scale NN applications [11] with pseudo-crossbar architecture (Fig.11(a)). Fig.11(b) shows the measured and estimated output of multiply and accumulate (MAC) operation of fabricated $2 \times 2$ arrays. The SE all FE-FinFETs based NN had an inference accuracy of $97.91\%$ . On the other hand, training of all FE-FinFET based NN without SE failed (Fig.12(b)). Fig.12(c) shows the required write-cycle for training operation for various NN architecture, which suggests an endurance above $10^{10}$ is required for implementing complex NN algorithm in hardware. Finally, the benchmarking (Table I) reveals that the performance of the devices reported in this paper outperforms other state-of-art MLC FE-FinFETs in terms of programming speed, power consumption, endurance and device area.

Conclusion: Improvement of HZO-silicon interface proves to be a pivotal step in achieving high reliability deeply scaled FE-FinFETs. This work shows that with the aid of surface engineering MLC can be facilitated in FE-FinFETs along with $10^{11}$ endurance, extrapolated 10-year retention at $85^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ which is a key requirement for in-situ training of neural networks.
