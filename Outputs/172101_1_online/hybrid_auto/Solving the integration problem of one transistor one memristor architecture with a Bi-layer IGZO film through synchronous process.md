# Solving the integration problem of one transistor one memristor architecture with a Bi-layer IGZO film through synchronous process

Che-Chia Chang,1 Po-Tsun Liu,2,a) Chen-Yu Chien,2 and Yang-Shun Fan2

1 Department of Electronics Engineering and Institute of Electronics, National Chiao Tung University, Hsinchu 30010, Taiwan   
2 Department of Photonics and Institute of Electro-Optical Engineering, National Chiao Tung University, Hsinchu 30010, Taiwan

(Received 24 December 2017; accepted 31 March 2018; published online 23 April 2018)

This study demonstrates the integration of a thin film transistor (TFT) and resistive random-access memory (RRAM) to form a one-transistor-one-resistor (1T1R) configuration. With the concept of the current conducting direction in RRAM and TFT, a triple-layer stack design of Pt/InGaZnO/ $\mathrm { A l } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 3 }$ is proposed for both the switching layer of RRAM and the channel layer of TFT. This proposal decreases the complexity of fabrication and the numbers of photomasks required. Also, the robust endurance and stable retention characteristics are exhibited by the 1T1R architecture for promising applications in memory-embedded flat panel displays. Published by AIP Publishing. https://doi.org/10.1063/1.5020583

Resistive random-access memory (RRAM), being one of the promising emerging memories, has advantages of high speed, simple structure, excellent endurance, and stability. These remarkable features make RRAM viable in the next generation non-volatile memory (NVM) applications.1–5 Besides commonly used materials such as hafnium oxide, tantalum oxide, and titanium oxide as resistive switching (RS) layers in RRAM for applications in conventional IC technology, indium-gallium-zinc-oxide (IGZO) has attracted a lot of attention in a wide range of oxide-based electronics6–10 in recent years due to their superior advantages of low cost, good uniformity for large area deposition, and multiple device function ability.12,13 These also allow IGZO-based electronic devices to be prospering in the applications of display circuits and potential development in non-volatile memory such as IGZO-based RRAM.7,8,11 On the other hand, for the sake of reducing power consumption in future display technology, embedded memory in the display circuit is a key method to solve the issue. Conventional memory-in-pixel (MIP) technology is constructed by adding an static random-access memory (SRAM) based circuit, which requires several CMOS based electronic units and sacrifices a particular area in every pixel.14–16 Another alternate method is adopting nonvolatile memory (NVM) devices into display pixel units and peripheral electronic systems such as in radio-frequency identification (RFID) and image sensors to reduce power consumption.17–20

Based on the merits of the IGZO material and the importance of memory-embedded display circuits, the integration of one IGZO-based thin film transistor (TFT) and IGZO-based RRAM using the simultaneous process for forming one-transistor-one-resistor (1T1R) architecture can effectively reduce the complexity and numbers of masks required for the process. In the 1T1R architecture, IGZO TFT as an access transistor plays a role in bringing the image information in terms of bias or current and triggers the RRAM cell as a storage element to

a different memorial state. Meanwhile, the saturated drain current of TFT suppresses the current overshoot in RRAM, stabilizing the operation and functional ability of the device. With simple structure design of 1T1R into a display circuit array, image information can be efficiently stored in the memory unit and thus reduce the static power consumption when the panel is hold in the standby status. In this work, we propose the highly integration of IGZO-based TFT and RRAM for the 1T1R architecture and optimize its electrical performance for future advanced power-saving display applications.

A layer of the IGZO thin film was then deposited by RF sputtering at room temperature, serving as both the active region of RRAM and TFT devices. All the electrical measurements were made using a Keithley 4200 semiconductor characterization analyzer for the DC characteristic of the transistor and memory cells. In order to avoid any external measurement factors that might interfere with the results of analysis, the voltage step used for every measurement was fixed at 0.05 V, and at each step, the measured current range was set at 1 pA with the selection of a “Normal” speed setup in Keithley 4200.

The critical issue for the realization of the 1T1R configuration is that the operating current of both the memory unit and transistor is necessary to be at a nearly identical level. Moreover, for the purpose of simultaneous fabrication of RRAM and TFT, the optimized film deposition condition of IGZO requires rigorous examination to be favorable for the switching layer and channel material of RRAM and TFT, respectively. The HRS can be found in the trend that the resistance state raises up as the oxygen gas flow increases during the IGZO deposition process. The resistance state for LRS is typically dominant at the localized filaments connected between the top and the bottom electrodes, and the trend in this work is not obvious. In comparison to LRS, the background property of the IGZO film can play a role in HRS since the filament is ruptured, which would make the case more similar to the initial state, and hence, both of them show a similar trend in the resistance change. The role of oxygen vacancy also indicated to be non-lattice oxygen may influence the initial conductivity of the IGZO film. The ratio of lattice and nonlattice oxygen is calculated from the integrated area for both peaks.30–34 As the oxygen concentration increases in a higher O2/Ar proportion during IGZO sputter-deposition, the ion bombardment effect will be more remarkable and lead to an increase in the amount of non-lattice oxygen vacancies but lower conductivity of the IGZO thin film.31–33,35–38 Another critical reason for the resistance change is shown in Table II, showing the atomic ratio for each element in the IGZO film by XPS analysis. It is found that with the increasing oxygen gas flow rate, the ratio of oxygen (O) in the IGZO film decreases and the ratio of Ga and Zn increases. Zn and Ga in IGZO play the role of stabilizing the IGZO film in the amorphous type, and Ga suppresses the excess carrier concentration. It was reported that the resistivity of the IGZO film can have a dramatic increase with the increasing Ga ratio.26,27 Moreover, the ratio of In, which is the dominant factor to control the IGZO film under high conductivity, shifted to lower values for those of IGZO films with increasing oxygen gas flow in comparison to the condition of 0% of oxygen gas. The best process condition for the manufacture of bilayer IGZO RRAM is the film with 20% $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ gas flow while considering the switching window, operation voltage, and endurance stability. However, it is not the best case for the IGZO TFT as shown in Fig. 3. The characteristics of the IGZO TFT device with the 20% $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ gas flow rate show lower switching current, higher threshold voltage, and worse subthreshold swing as compared to the TFT device with the 0% $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ flow rate. This degradation leads to serious problems for the integration of IGZO-based RRAM and TFT. The low operation current of IGZO TFT with the 20% $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ flow rate limits the feasibility for IGZO TFT to operate IGZO RRAM in the case of 1T1R integration since the driving current of RRAM comes from the drain current of the TFT device. Therefore, in this work, a triple layer design for both the switching layer of RRAM and the channel layer of TFT is proposed on the purpose to efficiently achieve the requirement of the integration. The schematic diagram for the triple layer 1T1R architecture is shown in Fig. 4(a). The IGZO film with 0% $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ process flow was deposited first and plays the role as the front channel of the transistor, for the purpose to transport current mainly through this IGZO film and hence achieve high operation current of the TFT device. A second IGZO film with 20% $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ process flow is deposited to retain the switching capability of the RRAM device since the IGZO RRAM with the 0% $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ flow condition does not work. A thin $\mathrm { { A l } } _ { 2 } \mathrm { { O } } _ { 3 }$ is finally grown as a barrier layer and a passivation layer for RRAM and TFT, respectively. This design is beneficial for different conduction directions of current transport for IGZO TFT and the IGZO RRAM cell, respectively, in the 1T1R architecture as shown in the arrow of Fig. 4(a). The entire operation and the performance of the 1T1R architecture can be thereby improved.

The switching result for triple-layer IGZO TFT and RRAM is shown in Figs. 4(b) and 4(c), respectively. As can be seen in Fig. 4(b), the triple-layer design benefits the TFT device with obvious enhancement in operation current. It is attributed to that the main current transport drops on the IGZO layer with 0% $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ gas flow, while the switching behavior of triple-layer RRAM does not have a distinct change as compared to the previous bilayer RRAM device. The operation polarity is reversed for triple-layer and bilayer IGZO RRAM due to the swap of applying bias from ITO to the Pt electrode. Finally, a demonstration of 1T1R operation is shown in Fig. 5. Figure 5(a) shows the typical bipolar RS characteristics of the 1T1R architecture, and the inset shows the electrical forming process. For the operation of 1T1R, the source electrode of TFT is common, and voltage bias is applied on the top electrode of RRAM, while the gate voltage of 10 V is constantly given to supply sufficient driving current. For the initial forming process, the positive voltage sweeps to around 9 V to activate the reversible resistive switching behaviors of the fresh memory devices into LRS. In the later set process, the positive sweeping voltage is applied on the top electrode from 0 to 5 V, and the device is triggered to LRS at the set voltage around 2 V. With a negative voltage sweeping from 0 to 5 V, the device is reset back to the high resistance state. In this work, a demonstration of the 1T1R architecture with simultaneous fabrication of the memory unit and transistor is presented. The optimized structure design is proposed to realize the feasibility of the 1T1R integration architecture. The triple-layer structure design enhances current transport in the IGZO TFT device and maintains the RS behavior of the RRAM cell, which enables the stable and smooth resistance change in the 1T1R configuration during DC cycling with robust retention characteristics up to $1 0 ^ { 4 }$ s. The convenience of this simultaneous process simplified the complexity of fabrication and reduces the number of masks required. It is noted that typically the programming time for display circuits is within order of microseconds, which is

also an available range to operate filamentary-based RRAM. Even in the device scaling-down case, the change in the current level of IGZO RRAM for LRS operation is not remarkable due to the filamentary-based conducting mechanism. On the other hand, it is usually difficult to satisfy all the requirements of optimized operation for both IGZO-based TFT and IGZO-based RRAM under the same IGZO deposition conditions since the current transport mechanisms for TFT and RRAM are quite different. Therefore, in this work, the design of bilayer IGZO is used to address the problem by combining both process conditions suitable for RRAM and the TFT device together. The proposed method is beneficial for the entire operation and the performance of 1T1R architecture regardless of the device dimension. It is believed that the demonstration in this work brings the potential for realizing the IGZO based 1T1R architecture and further application in the display systems for embedded memory related circuit design.
