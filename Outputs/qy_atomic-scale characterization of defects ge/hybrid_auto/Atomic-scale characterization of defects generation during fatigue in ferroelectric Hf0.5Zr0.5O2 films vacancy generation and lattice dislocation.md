# Atomic-scale characterization of defects generation during fatigue in ferroelectric Hf0.5Zr0.5O2 films: vacancy generation and lattice dislocation

Yunzhe Zheng1†, Yonghui Zheng1†, Zhaomeng Gao2,3, Jun-Hui Yuan4, Yan Cheng1*, Qilan Zhong1, Tianjiao Xin1, Yiwei Wang1, Cheng Liu1, Yaru Huang1, Rong Huang1, Xiangshui Miao4, Kan-Hao Xue4*, Hangbing Lyu2,3*

# Abstract

For the first time, we directly observed the lattice dislocation and monoclinic (m-) phase formation in ferroelectric $H f _ { 0 . 5 } Z r _ { 0 . 5 } O _ { 2 }$ (HZO) films during fatigue, through the spherical aberration (Cs)-corrected transmission electron microscopy (TEM) technique. The main observations are: 1. More oxygen vacancies (Vo) tend to be generated when the orthorhombic (o-) phase polar axis is close to the out-of-plane direction (parallel to the electrical field); 2. The o-phase with large grain size tends to fragment with lattice dislocation and m-phase formation by martensitic-like transformation; 3. At the interface of m-/o-structure, the Vo formation energies are lowered. This work provides fundamental understanding on the defect generation mechanism of HZO film at the atomic-level, laying a solid foundation to further optimization and commercialization of the ferroelectric HZO devices.

# Introduction

As a typical high-k material, hafnium oxide (HfO2) has attracted great interests due to the discovery of ferroelectricity in its doped films [1-3]. The 1:1 mixture of HfO2 and ZrO2 $( \mathrm { H f } _ { 0 . 5 } \mathrm { Z r } _ { 0 . 5 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ , HZO) with electrode cap (e.g. TiN) after rapid thermal process (RTP) manifests excellent polarization characteristics for logic and memory devices applications [4- 6]. The polarization origin of HZO thin film is considered as the ferroelectric o-phase with $P c a 2 _ { 1 }$ space group [7,8]. For convenience, $P c a 2 _ { 1 }$ 1 is referred to as Pbc21 below, for the match of a-, b-, and c-axis directions to other phases. Fig.1 shows the polar $_ { 0 - } P b c 2 _ { 1 }$ atomic structure of HZO with arrows indicating the off-centered positions of those oxygen atoms in cyan color $( \mathrm { O _ { I I } } )$ . The fatigue and subsequent breakdown of hafnia-based device usually have been explained as the generation of ${ \mathrm { V o } } ,$ which will cause leakage filaments under electrical field cycles [9]. Calculations predict that the polarization switching can create Vo [10]. However, experimental evidences for how and from where it should occur are still lacking. In this work, we investigated HZO ferroelectric thin films grown by highly matured atomic layer deposition (ALD) technique and experimentally confirmed that the amount of Vo produced by polarization switching is related to grain orientations. Crystal grains could be broken up with electrical cycles. In addition, the m-structure can form inside the o-grain by martensitic-like transformation. All of this facilitates the creation of Vo, which is the important cause of fatigue and breakdown.

# Experimental

The process scheme of our metal-ferroelectric-metal (MFM) capacitor is shown in Fig.2. The 10, 15 nm HZO thin films were deposited on 10 nm-thick TiN bottom electrode by ALD at 280°C. The Hf:Zr ratio was controlled by alternating deposition of HfO2 and $Z \mathrm { r } { \bf O } _ { 2 } .$ . Subsequently, the top TiN electrode layer was sputtered and patterned for polarization-voltage (P-V) measurement. Finally, the as-fabricated TiN/HZO/TiN capacitors were annealed at 500°C for 30 s in a nitrogen atmosphere for HZO crystallization. After different electrical operations, the TiN/HZO/TiN capacitors were processed into cross-section samples, after which the Cs-corrected TEM and scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) experiments were conducted for atomic characterization. As shown in Fig.3a, the cross-section STEM high-angle annular dark-field (HAADF) image exhibits the TiN/HZO/TiN stack with an o-phase grain. Zooming into the dashed cyan square in Fig.3a, the STEM annular bright-field (ABF) image in Fig.3b reveals that the o-phase grain possesses the Pbc21 symmetry with off-centred oxygen atomic columns, which is considered as the origin of ferroelectricity in hafnia-based materials.

# Results and discussion

# A. Oxygen vacancy generation and crystal orientation

Hafnia-based ferroelectric capacitors usually undergo a wakeup-fatigue-breakdown process under continuous switching cycles. To confirm the properties in TiN/HZO(10, 15 nm)/TiN ferroelectric capacitors, planar devices with 50×50 $\mu \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 }$ TiN top electrode were characterized. Fig.4 shows the properties of ferroelectric sandwich capacitors with different HZO thicknesses, involving the tendency of 2Pr and leakage current to cycles under 3.5 MV/cm, P-V loops, C-V curves, and I-V curves. Although the Pr values of HZO with 10, 15 nmthick vary, it can be clearly seen that all electrical trends are similar at different thicknesses. Both 10 and 15nm HZO films show obvious ferroelectricity and the leakage current is generally increasing for the whole wake-up, fatigue, and breakdown processes, which is attribute to the generation of Vo produced by repetitively switching. The reduced capacitance with cycling is a sign of dielectric constant reduction, implying that m-phase may be generated during cycling [11]. Considering the grain orientation of polycrystalline HZO grains prepared by ALD is random, in general there should be a varied

angle between the o-phase polar axis with the electrical field. Crystal grains with different orientations were then selected to investigate their Vo content variation after electrical operation. Fig.5a-5d show the grain whose polar axis points near the outof-plane direction, i.e., the direction of electrical field. Fig.5e-5h show the situation that the polar axis is parallel to the inplane direction, i.e., the vertical direction of electrical field. First of all, the cross-section STEM-HAADF images exhibit the o-phase grains projected along [100] (Fig.5a) and [001] (Fig.5e) zone axes, respectively. Zooming into the dashed cyan square in Fig.5a, 5e, the STEM-ABF images reveal its Pbc21 o-phase nature with distinct polarization directions, manifesting as the angles of 30o  (Fig.5b) and 90o  (Fig.5f) respectively between [001] polar axis and the electrical field direction. Furthermore, the coercive voltage of polar o-phase grains increases as the angle varies from 0° to 90°. In this case, the o-phase grains with small angle are more involved in ferroelectric switching, which may result in different Vo concentrations in o-phase grains. This point was confirmed by electron energy loss spectra (EELS) as shown in Fig.5c, 5g by detecting the plasma peaks. The peak at 16.4 eV labeled as A corresponds to the valence plasma excitation, while the peak at 26 eV labeled as B corresponds to the O 2s to Hf 5d excitation. Increasing peak B intensity implies Vo generation [12]. According to the EELS spectra, three points of agreement were reached that the peak B in Fig.5c is higher than peak A, which is reversed in Fig.5g. These results illustrate that more Vo are generated when the polar axis is closer to the electrical field direction. Polarization switching is closely related to the formation of Vo. Fig.5d, 5h show the schematic of the relation between polar axis of ophase crystal grain and the generation Vo content.

# B. Grain fragmentation and m-phase formation

Fig.6 shows the statistical results of grain size distribution of 15 nm-thick HZO films in lengh of ~1.2 μm (~100 grains) before (pristine) and after (woken-up and fatigued) electrical operation. The proportion of grains larger than 15 nm in size decreases obviously with cycling. Fig.7 gives the stitched typical TEM images of HZO films at different states. In the field of view, the pristine state contains 11 grains, which became 14 after wake up (104 cycles) and 17 after fatigue (107 cycles). This indicates that with the electrical field operation, the grains are fragmented. Hence, the cross-section STEM-HAADF image after electrical field cycles at wake-up state was captured as Fig.8a, exposing the inside information of o-grain projected along [010] zone axis, showing the formation of mdislocation. The white dotted lines correspond to the interfaces of m-/o-lattice, and m-structure is filled with purple background. Fig.8b, 8c show the zooming images acquired from the green and blue dashed squares in Fig.8a respectively, clearly exhibiting the martensitic-like o-to-m transformation with (100) slip plane along <001> direction. In Fig.9a, it can be seen that m-phase expands in the o-grain after cycles at fatigue state by inspecting the STEM-HAADF image projected along [010] direction. Fig.9b, 9c show the zooming image of m-/ointerfaces. It follows that electrical field operation transforms the polar o-phase into the nonpolar m-phase, leading to fatigue.

# C. Fatigue and breakdown mechanism analysis

Further density functional theory (DFT) calculations confirm that the m-/o-interface is prone to Vo generation, as shown in Fig.10 and Tab.1. The m-/o-interface contains two kinds of inequivalent oxygen sites, whose Vo formation energies are 6.193/6.189 eV (O1/O3) and 6.672/6.695 eV (O2/O4), taking the oxygen chemical potential as that of half the oxygen gas. In m-phase, the Vo formation energies are 6.561 eV (O5, tetra coordination oxygen) and 6.860/6.759 eV (O6/O7, triple coordination oxygen), respectively. These energies in o-phase are 6.590 eV (O8, tetra coordinate oxygen) and 6.818/6.854 eV (O9/O10, triple coordination oxygen, i.e., polarized oxygen). Therefore, the Vo formation energies of O1/O3 at the interface are noticeably lower than that in m-/ophase, indicating that Vo are more easily formed at the m-/ointerface. Accordingly, Fig.11 gives the schematic of o-to-m transformation and Vo generation mechanism with electrical field cycles. At the pristine state, Vo are relatively uniform, concentrated at the interface (Fig.11a). Polarization switching under electrical field operation yields lattice dislocation as shown in Fig.11b. The lower free energy of m-phase in contrast to o-phase acts as a driving force of o-to-m transformation (Fig.11c), especially in the case of large grains. Vo has the preference to emerge at the m-/o-interface. Once the Vo concentration reaches a certain level, permanently breakdown would occur (Fig.11d).

# Conclusion

In summary, we disclosed the atomic-level defect generation mechanism of HZO ferroelectric thin film under bipolar electrical field. Along with polarization switching, Vo were more easily generated in the o-phase grain with orientation parallel to the electrical field. Moreover, lattice dislocations were observed in o-phase grain with large grain size, accompanied by m-structure formation inside the o-grain. As the cycling goes on, the m-phase could expand and cause fatigue. The formation energy of Vo at the m-/o-interface is lower than that in bulk. These observations provide solid evidence on understanding the fundamental mechanism of fatigue and breakdown phenomenon in hafnia-based ferroelectric materials.
